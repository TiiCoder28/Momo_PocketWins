# Product Principles

These principles guide PocketWins product, UX, data, and AI decisions.

## 1. Personalised, not generic

PocketWins should become more useful as it learns from a user's own financial history, goals, choices, and progress.

The system should avoid repetitive advice such as "spend less" or "save more" when more specific insight is available.

## 2. Compare users primarily with themselves

The most important benchmark is the user's own historical behaviour.

Good:

> Your unplanned spending has decreased for two consecutive months.

Less useful:

> People your age spend less than you.

## 3. Reward progress, not perfection

PocketWins should recognise improvement even when a user has not reached an ideal target.

Examples:

- reducing overspend from R600 to R300 is progress
- saving R20 after previously saving R0 is progress
- avoiding one regretted purchase is progress

## 4. No financial shaming

The product should not label users as irresponsible or morally judge purchases.

Possible user-facing classifications include:

- Planned
- Flexible
- Unplanned
- Impulse
- Regretted
- Avoided purchase
- Positive personal win

Where possible, users should participate in labelling subjective behaviour rather than having AI make moral judgments on their behalf.

## 5. AI explains; deterministic systems calculate

Important numbers and trends should be calculated by the backend or analytics layer.

Examples:

- percentage reduction in overspend
- category totals
- streak length
- remaining safe-to-spend amount
- month-over-month changes

The AI layer can then translate those verified insights into clear, supportive language.

## 6. Saving is voluntary

"Pay Yourself" should always be optional.

A user may have made a positive choice without having money available to move into savings. PocketWins should celebrate the behaviour without pressuring the user to save an amount they cannot afford.

## 7. Positive reinforcement matters

PocketWins should recognise good decisions, consistency, and behavioural improvement—not only problems.

Examples:

> You stayed within your transport plan 7 of the last 8 weeks.

> You reduced your takeaway spending for three consecutive months.

> You paid your future self R95 this week.

## 8. The user's goals define success

Different users care about different things. One may want to reduce takeaway spending, another may want to build emergency savings, another may want to reward exercise or study habits.

PocketWins should allow personal rules and goals rather than imposing one universal financial philosophy.

## 9. Keep the product lightweight

PocketWins is a MoMo Mini App, not a full accounting suite.

The experience should remain fast, understandable, mobile-first, and focused on everyday decisions.

## 10. Privacy and trust are product features

Behavioural finance can involve sensitive personal information. The app should collect only what is needed, explain how data is used, and avoid unnecessary inference about a user's life.

Personalisation should feel useful—not invasive.
